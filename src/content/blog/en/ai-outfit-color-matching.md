## The Color Question Everyone Asks

"What colors look good on me?" It's one of the most googled style questions — and for good reason. The right colors near your face can make your skin look clear, your eyes bright, and your whole appearance more vibrant. The wrong colors do the opposite: they make you look tired, sallow, or washed out.

The difference isn't subtle. Imagine wearing a pure white shirt versus an off-white cream — for some people, one will make them look fresh and awake while the other makes them look ill. That's the power of understanding your personal color palette.

---

## Why Standard Advice Doesn't Work

Generic rules like "wear black if you want to look slim" or "pastels are for spring" ignore the fundamental issue: **colors interact with your individual skin tone, undertone, and contrast level**. What flatters a Deep Winter will drain a Light Spring. What energizes a Warm Autumn will overwhelm a Cool Summer.

This is why two people can try on the same red dress and look completely different. One glows; the other disappears behind the fabric. It's not about the garment — it's about the interaction between fabric color and skin.

## The Three Categories of Colors

Every AI color preview divides colors into three buckets:

### Brightening Colors (Your Power Colors)
These make your skin glow, your eyes pop, and your overall appearance more vibrant. When you wear these, people notice YOU — not the clothing. These are your go-to colors for important meetings, dates, and photos.

### Neutral Colors (Safe & Versatile)
These neither help nor hurt. They're workable for everyday wear and mix well with your power colors. Think of them as your wardrobe foundation — the pieces that connect everything else.

### Draining Colors (Best Avoided Near Face)
These make your skin look dull, emphasize dark circles, or create an unflattering cast. You don't need to ban them entirely — just keep them away from your face (use them in bottoms, bags, or shoes instead).

## Practical Tips for Shopping With Your Colors

1. **Save your palette to your phone** — screenshot your AI report and reference it when shopping
2. **Start with basics in your neutral colors** — build your wardrobe foundation with flattering neutrals
3. **Add statement pieces in your power colors** — these are the pieces that get compliments
4. **Don't throw everything away** — if you love a "draining" color, wear it as a bottom or accessory
5. **Remember: lighting matters** — colors look different under store lighting vs. natural light

## Beyond Clothing: Colors Everywhere

Your personal color palette applies to more than just clothing:

- **Makeup:** Foundation undertone, lipstick shades, eyeshadow palette
- **Hair color:** Which highlights/lowlights suit your natural coloring
- **Jewelry:** Gold vs. silver vs. rose gold
- **Home decor:** The colors that make you look best in photos at home
- **Car color:** Yes, some people take it that far (and it works)
