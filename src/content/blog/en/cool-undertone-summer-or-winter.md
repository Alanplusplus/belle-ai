## The short answer

A cool undertone tells you the temperature of your best colors. It does not tell you your season, because both Summer and Winter are cool. What separates them is intensity:

- **Summer** is cool and soft. Your skin, hair and eyes tend to blend together, and colors look best slightly muted or dusty.
- **Winter** is cool and clear. Your features tend to stand apart from each other, and crisp, saturated colors look best.

One quick check: if a vivid color such as fuchsia, true red or cobalt makes you look sharper and more awake, lean Winter. If it arrives before your face does and a dustier version looks right, lean Summer.

## The six cool-leaning seasons

In the 12-season system, three Summers and three Winters lean cool. This table uses the same definitions as our palette pages.

| Season | Undertone | Depth | Clarity | Contrast |
|--------|-----------|-------|---------|----------|
| [Light Summer](/color-palette/light-summer) | Cool (pink, rosy) | Very light | Medium | Low |
| [Cool Summer](/color-palette/cool-summer) (True Summer) | Strongly cool | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| [Soft Summer](/color-palette/soft-summer) | Cool-neutral | Medium | Low (muted) | Low |
| [Cool Winter](/color-palette/cool-winter) (True Winter) | Strongly cool | Medium-deep | High | High |
| [Deep Winter](/color-palette/deep-winter) | Cool-neutral | Very deep | High | High |
| [Bright Winter](/color-palette/bright-winter) | Cool-neutral | Medium | Very high | Very high |

Other analysts name and split these types differently, so the same person can get different labels from different people. The useful part is the pattern: Summers sit lower on clarity and contrast, and Winters sit higher.

## Three questions that separate Summer from Winter

**1. How strong is your contrast?**
Look at your skin, hair and eyes in daylight. If they are close in lightness and blend together, that points to Summer. If there is a clear step between them, for example very dark hair with light skin and clear eyes, that points to Winter.

A quick hint: take a daylight photo with no filter and convert it to black and white. If everything melts together, your contrast is low. If you see clear light and dark steps, it is high. A photo is only a hint, so confirm it with the tests below.

**2. How clear or muted is your coloring?**
Soft, blended eye color and hair with a slightly ashy cast tend to lean Summer. Crisp, defined eye color and rich, clear hair tend to lean Winter. Clarity is the main thing that separates Soft Summer from Bright Winter.

**3. How deep is your overall coloring?**
Very light overall coloring points to Light Summer. Very deep overall coloring points to Deep Winter. Medium coloring could be Cool Summer, Soft Summer, Cool Winter or Bright Winter, so clarity and contrast decide it.

Two common traps: dark hair does not automatically mean Winter, and fair skin does not automatically mean Summer. Cool Summer hair is often a medium-dark brown that looks soft rather than stark, and Bright Winter can have fair skin with dark hair and striking eyes.

## Two drape tests to run in daylight

Sit by a window with a bare face and hair pulled back. Hold each fabric under your chin for 10 to 15 seconds. Judge what the color does to your face, not whether you like the color. Look for even skin, clear eyes and softer shadows.

**Test A: vivid against soft.** Compare a vivid fuchsia with a muted dusty blue or dusty rose.
- If the fuchsia makes you look sharp and alive, that points to Cool Winter or Bright Winter.
- If the fuchsia overpowers you and the dusty color looks harmonious, that points to Cool Summer, Soft Summer or Light Summer.

**Test B: black and white against softer neutrals.** Compare pure black and crisp white with charcoal and soft white.
- If pure black looks crisp and you are not overwhelmed, that points to Winter.
- If charcoal looks more polished and black looks heavy, that points to Summer.

**Test C: depth.** Compare a midnight navy that is almost black with a medium cool blue.
- If only the very dark navy looks natural, that points to Deep Winter.
- If the medium cool blue looks sharper, you are more likely a Cool Winter, a Bright Winter or a Summer type.

## Quick decision guide

- Everything is soft and blended, and dusty colors look best: **Soft Summer**
- Very light and delicate, and cool pastels look best: **Light Summer**
- Clearly cool, medium depth, moderate contrast, and soft navy and dusty blue look best: **Cool Summer**
- Bold and strongly cool, and black, pure white and hot pink look good on you: **Cool Winter**
- Very dark and dramatic, and only deep jewel tones look natural: **Deep Winter**
- Vivid and electric, with very high contrast and striking eyes: **Bright Winter**

## Mistakes that lead to the wrong season

- **Using silver and gold as the deciding test.** Silver looking better only confirms that you lean cool. It cannot separate Summer from Winter.
- **Typing by hair color alone.** Contrast and clarity matter more than whether your hair is dark.
- **Testing in warm indoor light.** Warm bulbs add yellow to everyone. See [how lighting affects your colors](/blog/how-lighting-affects-your-colors).
- **Choosing the palette you like better.** Judge the effect on your face, not the color itself.
- **Testing with only one kind of color.** Muted colors can make both types look dull, and neon colors can make both look off. Compare vivid with soft so the difference shows.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a cool undertone Summer or Winter?**
It can be either. Both Summer and Winter have cool coloring. Summer is cool and soft, with features that blend together. Winter is cool and clear, with higher contrast and more saturated colors. Contrast and clarity separate the two, not undertone.

**Does silver or gold jewelry tell me if I'm Summer or Winter?**
No. Silver looking better only confirms that you lean cool, and both Summer and Winter do. To separate them, compare soft colors against vivid ones and look at how strong your contrast is.

**Can I have dark hair and be a Summer?**
Yes. Dark hair does not make you a Winter. Cool Summer hair is often a medium-dark brown that looks soft rather than stark. Winter is defined by contrast and clarity across skin, hair and eyes, not by hair color alone.

**What if I sit between two cool seasons?**
That is common. Navy and cool gray appear in both the Cool Summer and Cool Winter palettes, so you can wear them while you test. Use the side-by-side comparison pages to find the one test that separates your two types.

**How accurate is an AI color analysis for telling Summer from Winter?**
It is an AI's visual judgment of one photo, so lighting, camera settings and makeup can change the result. If two photos give you different seasons, treat the AI result as one input and rely on the fabric test in daylight.
