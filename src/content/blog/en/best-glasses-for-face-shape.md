## Why Face Shape Matters When Choosing Glasses

Choosing glasses isn't just about vision correction or sun protection — it's one of the most visible accessories you wear on your face every single day. The right frames can balance your proportions, highlight your best features, and even make your face appear slimmer or more symmetrical. The wrong ones? They can overwhelm delicate features or emphasize proportions you'd rather not draw attention to.

The principle is straightforward: **your frames should contrast with your face shape**, not mimic it. Round faces benefit from angular frames that add definition. Angular faces are softened by rounded or curved designs. But the details matter more than most guides tell you.

---

## The 6 Face Shapes and Their Best Frames

### Oval Face

The oval face is the most versatile — balanced proportions with a slightly narrower forehead and jaw. Almost any frame style works, but some choices are still better than others.

**Best frames:** Walnut-shaped, aviator, cat-eye, rectangular, browline
**What to look for:** Frames as wide as or wider than the broadest part of your face. Balanced proportions that don't disrupt your natural symmetry.
**What to avoid:** Oversized frames that overwhelm your face, or very narrow frames that make your face look wider than it is.

### Round Face

Round faces have similar width and length, with soft angles at the forehead, cheekbones, and jaw. The goal is to add angles and elongate.

**Best frames:** Rectangular, square, geometric, browline, cat-eye with angular lines
**What to look for:** Frames with sharp angles and straight lines. Wider-than-tall proportions. A frame that sits high on your nose to elongate.
**What to avoid:** Round or circular frames (they echo your face shape), small frames that make your face look larger, rimless frames that add no structure.

### Square Face

Strong jawline, broad forehead, and angular features define the square face. Soften those angles with curves.

**Best frames:** Round, oval, rimless, aviator, cat-eye with curves
**What to look for:** Curved or rounded shapes that contrast your angular jaw. Thin frames that don't add bulk. Slightly wider frames to balance the jaw.
**What to avoid:** Square or boxy frames that compete with your jaw, heavy or thick frames that add weight, small frames that emphasize width.

### Heart Face (Inverted Triangle)

Wide forehead, prominent cheekbones, narrow chin. The goal is to draw attention downward and balance the top-heaviness.

**Best frames:** Bottom-heavy frames, rimless, aviator, round
**What to look for:** Frames that are wider at the bottom. Light colors and thin rims on top. Frames with low-set temples.
**What to avoid:** Top-heavy frames, decorative browlines, cat-eyes that emphasize the forehead width.

### Diamond Face

Narrow forehead and jaw with wide cheekbones. This angular face benefits from softer shapes.

**Best frames:** Oval, cat-eye, rimless, browline
**What to look for:** Curved lines that soften cheekbones. Frames with detailing at the brow line. Slightly wider than your cheekbones.
**What to avoid:** Narrow frames that emphasize cheekbone width, boxy shapes that compete with angles.

### Oblong (Rectangle) Face

Longer than wide, with a long nose and sometimes a long chin. The goal is to add width and break up length.

**Best frames:** Oversized, round, square with depth, aviator, decorative temples
**What to look for:** Frames with more depth (height) to shorten the face visually. Bold colors and thick rims. Decorative side temples that add width.
**What to avoid:** Small narrow frames that make your face look longer, single-bridge styles that add length.

## Frame Color Based on Skin Tone

Beyond shape, color matters. Your frame color should complement your skin's undertone:

**Warm undertones:** Tortoiseshell, honey, warm brown, gold metal, cream, olive, warm red
**Cool undertones:** Black, gunmetal, silver, cool brown, blue, purple, rose gold
**Neutral undertones:** You're lucky — both warm and cool frame colors work. Try clear/crystal frames for a modern look.

## Tips for Your Next Eyewear Purchase

1. **Bring a friend or use AI** — it's hard to judge frames objectively when you're wearing them without your prescription lenses
2. **Your frame width should match your face width** — measure from temple to temple
3. **Your eyes should be centered** in the frame opening, not pushed to the edges
4. **Consider your lifestyle** — sporty frames for active people, statement frames for those who want a bold look
5. **Don't forget sunglasses** — the same face-shape rules apply, but you can be bolder with sun protection
