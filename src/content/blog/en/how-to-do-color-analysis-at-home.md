## Can You Do Color Analysis at Home?

Yes. While in-person professional draping remains the gold standard, you can get remarkably accurate results at home using a combination of self-assessment tests and AI technology. This guide walks you through the entire process step by step.

## What You'll Need

- A large window with indirect natural daylight
- A mirror
- White, off-white, silver, and gold fabric/paper/clothing
- A bare face (no makeup, remove glasses)
- Your phone camera
- Optional: white towel to wrap around your shoulders

## Step 1: Determine Your Undertone

Your undertone is the foundation of color analysis. It doesn't change with age, tanning, or weight fluctuation.

### The Vein Test

Look at the veins on the inside of your wrist in natural light:
- **Green/olive veins** → Warm undertone
- **Blue/purple veins** → Cool undertone
- **Both blue and green** → Neutral undertone

### The Jewelry Test

Hold gold jewelry next to silver jewelry against your skin:
- **Gold looks better** → Warm undertone
- **Silver looks better** → Cool undertone
- **Both look equally good** → Neutral undertone

### The White vs. Cream Test

Hold pure white paper and cream/ivory paper next to your face:
- **Pure white flatters** → Cool undertone (cream makes you look sallow)
- **Cream/ivory flatters** → Warm undertone (white makes you look washed out)
- **Both work** → Neutral undertone

### The Sun Response Test

How does your skin typically react to sun exposure?
- **Burns first, then tans golden** → Warm undertone
- **Burns easily, tans poorly or gets pink** → Cool undertone
- **Tans easily, rarely burns** → Warm (possibly deep)

## Step 2: Determine Your Depth

Depth refers to how light or dark your overall coloring is — considering skin, hair, and eyes together.

Stand in front of a mirror and assess:
- **Light:** Fair skin + blonde/light brown hair + light eyes → Light Spring or Light Summer
- **Deep:** Medium-dark to dark skin + dark hair + dark eyes → Deep Winter or Deep Autumn
- **Medium:** Everything in between → Could be any of the remaining 8 seasons

## Step 3: Determine Your Chroma

Chroma (also called clarity or saturation) measures how vivid or muted your natural coloring is.

### The Brightness Test

Hold a bright, vivid color (like electric blue or hot pink) next to your face, then a muted version of the same hue (dusty blue or mauve):

- **Bright looks better** — You're clear/bright (Bright Spring or Bright Winter)
- **Muted looks better** — You're soft/muted (Soft Summer or Soft Autumn)
- **Neither dominates** — Depth or temperature is your dominant characteristic

### Visual Contrast Assessment

Look at yourself without squinting:
- **Features are distinct and separate** (dark brows against light skin, bright eyes) → High contrast/Bright
- **Features blend gently together** (eyebrows similar to hair, subtle coloring) → Low contrast/Soft

## Step 4: DIY Draping Test

Now combine your findings:

### Warm + Light = Spring

Test these colors against your face:
- Peach, coral, warm green, golden yellow
- If these make your skin glow → You're likely a Spring type

### Cool + Light = Summer

Test:
- Lavender, dusty rose, sky blue, soft teal
- If these make you look fresh and balanced → Summer type

### Warm + Deep = Autumn

Test:
- Rust, olive, mustard, burnt orange, chocolate
- If these feel rich and grounding → Autumn type

### Cool + Deep = Winter

Test:
- True red, black, white, royal blue, hot pink
- If these make your features pop dramatically → Winter type

## Step 5: Narrow to Your Sub-Season

Once you've identified your main season, determine which sub-type you are:

**If you're Spring:**
- Light Spring → Lightness is most prominent (pastel warmth)
- Warm Spring → Warmth is most prominent (golden, toasty)
- Bright Spring → Clarity is most prominent (vivid, saturated)

**If you're Summer:**
- Light Summer → Lightness is most prominent (ethereal, soft)
- Cool Summer → Coolness is most prominent (blue-based)
- Soft Summer → Softness is most prominent (blended, gentle)

**If you're Autumn:**
- Soft Autumn → Softness is most prominent (muted, earthy)
- Warm Autumn → Warmth is most prominent (golden, rich)
- Deep Autumn → Depth is most prominent (intense, grounded)

**If you're Winter:**
- Deep Winter → Depth is most prominent (dark, dramatic)
- Cool Winter → Coolness is most prominent (icy, blue-based)
- Bright Winter → Clarity is most prominent (vivid contrast)

## Common Mistakes in DIY Color Analysis

### Mistake 1: Bad Lighting

Indoor artificial lighting has color casts (warm yellow from incandescent, cool blue from LED) that will skew every test. Always use natural daylight from a window — ideally on a cloudy day or facing north.

### Mistake 2: Wearing Makeup

Even "minimal" makeup (tinted moisturizer, concealer, lip balm with color) changes how colors interact with your skin. Start completely bare-faced.

### Mistake 3: Confusing Skin Color with Undertone

A person with very dark skin can have a cool undertone, and a person with very fair skin can have a warm undertone. Skin darkness (depth) and undertone are independent dimensions.

### Mistake 4: Letting Preferences Bias Results

You might love wearing black, but that doesn't mean you're a Winter. Focus on what makes your skin look healthy and radiant, not what you think looks "cool."

### Mistake 5: Testing With Patterned Fabric

Use solid colors only when draping. Patterns contain multiple colors that muddy the comparison.
