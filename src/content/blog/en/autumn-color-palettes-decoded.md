## You're an Autumn — But Which Kind?

So your color analysis came back Autumn. You've got warmth in your veins, richness in your coloring, and you look incredible in shades that remind people of forest floors, harvest markets, and golden hour.

But here's where it gets interesting — there are **three distinct Autumn sub-seasons** in the 12-season color analysis system, and they're more different than most people realize. A color that makes one Autumn type look radiant can make another look muddy or washed out.

I've seen this mistake so many times: someone discovers they're an Autumn, fills their closet with every shade of orange and brown they can find, and then wonders why some pieces make them glow while others make them look tired. The answer is almost always a sub-season mismatch.

In this guide, I'll walk you through **Deep Autumn**, **True Autumn**, and **Soft Autumn** — what makes each one unique, which colors are your best and worst, and exactly how to figure out which Autumn you actually are.

---

## What All Autumns Have in Common

Before we get into the differences, let's talk about what unites the Autumn family.

Every Autumn shares these core traits:

- **Warm undertone** — your skin has a golden, peachy, or warm-olive quality
- **Rich, saturated coloring** — there's a density and earthiness to your natural palette
- **Warm-toned eyes** — amber, warm brown, hazel with gold flecks, olive green, or warm green
- **Hair with warm tones** — auburn, chestnut, warm brown, copper, strawberry blonde, or dark brown with reddish highlights
- **Medium to high depth** — Autumns rarely look washed out; there's substance to their coloring

Think of the color energy of a New England fall: burnished gold, terracotta, deep olive, rich burgundy, warm chocolate. Autumn palettes capture that energy — they're warm, rich, and grounded.

The three sub-seasons differ based on their **secondary characteristic**:

| Sub-Season | Primary Quality | Secondary Quality | Think Of It As... |
|-----------|----------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Deep Autumn | Warm | High depth/richness | Autumn meets Winter |
| True Autumn | Warm | Medium warmth | The most "typical" Autumn |
| Soft Autumn | Warm | High mutedness | Autumn meets Summer |

Let's break each one down.

---

## Deep Autumn: The Dramatic Warmth

### Who They Are

Deep Autumns sit at the border between Autumn and Winter. They carry the warmth of Autumn but with a depth and intensity that other Autumns can't match. They're the people who can pull off rich, saturated colors that would overpower lighter Autumn types.

**Physical characteristics:**

- Skin: Medium to deep with warm-olive, golden-bronze, or rich warm-beige undertones
- Eyes: Dark warm brown, deep olive, dark hazel, or rich chocolate with amber flecks
- Hair: Dark brown with warm undertones, deep auburn, espresso, or black-brown
- Overall impression: Deep, warm, and high-contrast — there's an intensity to their coloring that commands attention

### Best Colors

Deep Autumn's palette is built around **warm, saturated colors with significant depth**:

- **Reds:** Burgundy, oxblood, dark tomato red, warm cranberry
- **Oranges:** Burnt sienna, deep terracotta, dark rust, spiced pumpkin
- **Greens:** Forest green, deep olive, dark teal (warm-leaning), hunter green
- **Browns:** Espresso, dark chocolate, warm mahogany, chestnut
- **Others:** Deep gold, dark turquoise, warm plum, eggplant, dark warm coral
- **Neutrals:** Dark chocolate brown, warm charcoal, deep camel, dark olive

### Colors to Avoid

- Pastel anything — baby pink, powder blue, light lavender
- Cool-toned brights: fuchsia, electric blue, cool magenta
- Icy whites and cool gray
- Light, washed-out colors: pale peach, light mint, soft lilac
- Neon or fluorescent shades

### Deep Autumn vs Dark Winter

This is one of the trickiest distinctions in color analysis. Both are deep and dramatic. The key: Deep Autumns have undeniable warmth — hold a warm rust next to a cool burgundy near your face. If the warm shade makes your skin glow and the cool one makes it look grayish, you're Deep Autumn. Dark Winters lean cool even in their depth.

## True Autumn: The Harvest Classic

### Who They Are

True Autumn (sometimes called Warm Autumn or Pure Autumn) is the most balanced, quintessential Autumn type. They embody everything people picture when they think "autumn colors" — warm, rich, earthy, and golden. These are the people who look born to wear that perfect camel coat or rust-colored sweater.

**Physical characteristics:**

- Skin: Warm beige, golden, peach-toned, or warm olive; often freckled or with a natural warm flush
- Eyes: Warm hazel, topaz brown, amber, golden-green, or warm olive
- Hair: Auburn, copper, warm chestnut, golden brown, or strawberry blonde — hair often has red or gold highlights in sunlight
- Overall impression: Undeniably warm, medium-depth, and golden — like they're always standing in golden hour light

### Best Colors

True Autumn's palette is the definitive **warm earth tone** collection:

- **Oranges:** Pumpkin, terracotta, burnt orange, warm coral, sienna
- **Reds:** Tomato red, warm brick red, dark salmon, rust
- **Greens:** Olive, moss green, khaki, warm sage, forest green (warm-cast)
- **Browns:** Camel, cognac, warm tan, saddle brown, cinnamon
- **Yellows:** Mustard, golden amber, dark gold, warm honey
- **Neutrals:** Camel, warm beige, coffee brown, warm ivory, cream

### Colors to Avoid

- Cool pinks: rose, mauve, cool berry
- Blue-based colors: royal blue, periwinkle, cobalt
- Silver and cool metallics
- Pure white (warm ivory is fine)
- Cool gray, charcoal, and black (looks disconnected from their warmth)
- Pastel or icy versions of any color

### True Autumn's Signature Move

True Autumns have a superpower that no other season can match: they own earth tones like nobody else. Colors that look boring on other people — khaki, olive, camel, rust — become **elevated and sophisticated** on True Autumns. Where a Summer would look drained in mustard, a True Autumn looks like they stepped out of a Vogue editorial.

## Soft Autumn: The Gentle Warmth

### Who They Are

Soft Autumns sit at the border between Autumn and Summer. They have warmth, but it's tempered — muted, gentle, and whisper-soft rather than bold. They're often the hardest Autumn to identify because their coloring is so subtle that they can be mistaken for Soft Summers.

**Physical characteristics:**

- Skin: Light to medium with a warm-neutral quality; can be olive-neutral, soft peach, or warm beige with low saturation
- Eyes: Soft hazel, grayed green, warm gray-blue, or muted brown with gold flecks
- Hair: Mousy brown with warm undertones, dark blonde, ashy golden brown, or medium brown that can't decide if it's warm or cool
- Overall impression: Muted, low contrast, and gently warm — as if someone applied a soft-focus filter to an Autumn palette

### Best Colors

Soft Autumn's palette is built on **muted, dusty warm tones**:

- **Greens:** Sage, muted olive, eucalyptus (warm-leaning), soft khaki, faded jade
- **Browns:** Taupe, warm mushroom, soft camel, light cocoa, fawn
- **Pinks/Reds:** Dusty rose (warm-cast), muted coral, salmon, warm mauve
- **Oranges:** Muted terracotta, soft peach, dusty apricot, light rust
- **Yellows:** Soft gold, wheat, light amber, champagne
- **Neutrals:** Warm greige, stone, oatmeal, soft camel, warm ivory

### Colors to Avoid

- Bright, saturated colors of any temperature
- Deep, heavy colors: black, dark navy, deep burgundy
- High-contrast combinations
- Neon or electric shades
- Cool, icy colors: ice blue, bright white, cool silver
- Pure primary colors: true red, sunshine yellow, cobalt blue

### Soft Autumn's Secret Weapon

Soft Autumns often feel frustrated because they think their best colors are "boring." But here's the truth: Soft Autumns create the most **effortlessly sophisticated** looks in the entire 12-season system. Their muted palette creates a tonal dressing effect that looks incredibly expensive. Think of a head-to-toe camel and cream outfit with sage accents — on a Soft Autumn, this looks like quiet luxury personified.

## How to Tell Which Autumn You Are

Still not sure? Here are three practical tests:

### Test 1: The Depth Test

Try these three colors next to your face in natural light:

- A **deep burgundy** or espresso (Deep Autumn)
- A **medium pumpkin** or warm terracotta (True Autumn)
- A **soft sage** or dusty camel (Soft Autumn)

Which depth level makes your features come alive without overpowering them?

### Test 2: The Saturation Test

Hold up three warm fabrics of the same color family:

- A **rich, saturated olive** (Deep Autumn)
- A **clear, medium olive** (True Autumn)
- A **dusty, muted olive** (Soft Autumn)

Your best saturation level reveals your sub-season more reliably than any quiz.

### Test 3: The Contrast Test

Look at your natural coloring in a mirror:

- Is there a **strong contrast** between your hair, skin, and eyes? → **Deep Autumn**
- Is there a **moderate, warm contrast** — clearly defined but not dramatic? → **True Autumn**
- Is there **very little contrast** — your features blend together softly? → **Soft Autumn**

## Practical Styling Tips by Sub-Season

### Deep Autumn Wardrobe Strategy

Build your wardrobe on a base of **dark chocolate, deep olive, and warm charcoal**. Your accent colors are burgundy, burnt orange, and forest green. Gold jewelry in rich tones (not too yellow or bright) is your signature. For makeup, lean into warm berry lips, bronze and copper eyeshadows, and warm bronzer for contour.

### True Autumn Wardrobe Strategy

Your base should be **camel, cognac, and warm beige**. Everything should feel warm and rich without being too dark or too light. Your accent colors are rust, olive, and mustard. Gold jewelry is your absolute best metal. For makeup, warm coral lips, terracotta blush, and amber/copper eyeshadows create that natural golden glow.

### Soft Autumn Wardrobe Strategy

Build on **taupe, oatmeal, and soft camel**. Keep everything muted and tonal — Soft Autumns look best in low-contrast, monochromatic outfits. Your accent colors are sage, dusty rose, and soft terracotta. Both matte gold and antique rose gold work beautifully. For makeup, think nude-peach lips, soft warm blush, and warm-toned neutrals with no shimmer.

## Autumn Metals and Jewelry Guide

Autumns are generally gold people, but the specific tone varies:

| Sub-Season | Best Metal | Also Works |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Deep Autumn | Rich antique gold, brass | Bronze, dark copper |
| True Autumn | Classic warm gold, copper | Brass, bronze |
| Soft Autumn | Matte gold, rose gold | Antique brass, soft copper |

Silver is a struggle for all Autumn types. If you love silver jewelry, opt for white gold with a warm cast or mixed-metal pieces instead.

## Key Takeaways

- **Deep Autumn:** The most intense of the Autumns. Rich, deep, and saturated warm colors. Borders Winter.
- **True Autumn:** The balanced center. Medium-depth, clearly warm, and earthy. The quintessential Autumn.
- **Soft Autumn:** The most muted of the Autumns. Dusty, gentle, and sophisticated warm-neutrals. Borders Summer.
- All Autumns share warm undertones and rich, grounded coloring — the difference is depth and saturation.
- When in doubt, try the depth test: your sub-season is revealed by how much color weight your features can carry without being overpowered.
